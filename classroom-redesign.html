Classroom Redesign

2025

A room redesigned with help from the students who use it.

I inherited a CS classroom that worked the way most middle school classrooms work. Bright fluorescents overhead, rows of identical tables, one open floor that students treated as either a hallway or an obstacle. Before I changed anything, I asked the students what they thought.

The survey was three questions. What about this room makes it hard to focus. What about this room makes it hard to work with other people. If you could change one thing, what would it be. The answers were remarkably consistent across sections. The lights were too bright. There was nowhere to actually sit with a group. And the robotics work, which involved kits and motors and field pieces, was happening on the same tables as everything else, so cleanup ate into class time and projects rarely got finished.

I rebuilt the room around those answers.

Lighting

Out went the fluorescents. In came warmer, dimmable task lighting over the work zones, with brighter direct lighting near the front for instruction. Students said the room felt calmer almost immediately.

Seating zones

The rows came apart into three zones. A soft-seating corner for reading and small-group conversation. Collaborative tables for group projects. Individual desks at the edges for focused work. Students pick where to sit based on what they're doing, and the room responds to them instead of the other way around.

The robotics arena

I carved out a permanent corner for robotics: a fixed field, organized kit storage, a workbench for assembly. Projects stay set up between classes. Students iterate over days, not minutes, and they finish things.

What I learned

The most useful thing about asking first was that it took the project out of my own taste. Most of the changes I would have prioritized on my own (more wall storage, a different desk layout) didn't appear in the survey. Most of what students asked for (warmer lights, a place to sit, a robotics zone that stayed assembled) wasn't on my own list. Listening before designing changed what got designed.
